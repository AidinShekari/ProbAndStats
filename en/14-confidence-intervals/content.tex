\PSsection{14.1}{Introduction: Why Point Estimates Aren’t Enough}

A point estimate (e.g., \ensuremath{\bar{X}} = 3.47) gives no information about \textbf{precision}. We need to quantify uncertainty:

``The true mean is estimated to be 3.47, \textbf{give or take} some amount.''

A \textbf{confidence interval} provides a range that, with a specified confidence level, captures the true parameter.

\PSsection{14.2}{Correct Interpretation of Confidence Intervals}

\PSsubsection{\PSyes{} CORRECT (Frequentist) Interpretation}

\begin{PSquote}

``If we repeat this experiment many times, each time computing a 95\% CI, then approximately 95\% of those intervals will contain the true parameter \ensuremath{\mu}.''

\end{PSquote}

The \textbf{procedure} has a 95\% success rate. Each specific interval either contains \ensuremath{\mu} or it doesn’t.

\PSsubsection{\PSno{} WRONG Interpretation}

\begin{PSquote}

{``There is a 95\% probability that \ensuremath{\mu} is inside this particular interval [2.1, 4.8].''}

\end{PSquote}

\textbf{Why this is wrong:} \ensuremath{\mu} is a fixed (non-random) number. It’s either in [2.1, 4.8] or not — there is no probability about it. The \textbf{interval} is random (varies from sample to sample), not the parameter.

\PSsubsection{Analogy}

Think of CIs like a fishing net:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Before you cast: 95\% chance your net will catch the fish (the procedure works 95\% of the time)
\item
  After you cast: the fish is either caught or not — no probability anymore
\item
  The fish (\ensuremath{\mu}) doesn’t move; your net (CI) does
\end{itemize}

\PSsection{14.3}{CI for Mean — Known Variance (Z-interval)}

\PSsubsection{Setup}

Data: X\textsubscript{1}, …, X\textsubscript{n} i.i.d. from N(\ensuremath{\mu}, \ensuremath{\sigma}\textsuperscript{2}) with \ensuremath{\sigma}\textsuperscript{2} \textbf{known}.

\PSsubsection{Derivation}

\ensuremath{\bar{X}} \textasciitilde{} N(\ensuremath{\mu}, \ensuremath{\sigma}\textsuperscript{2}/n), so Z = (\ensuremath{\bar{X}} - \ensuremath{\mu})/(\ensuremath{\sigma}/\ensuremath{\surd}n) \textasciitilde{} N(0,1)

P(-z\textsubscript{\ensuremath{\alpha}/2} \ensuremath{\le} Z \ensuremath{\le} z\textsubscript{\ensuremath{\alpha}/2}) = 1 - \ensuremath{\alpha}

Solving for \ensuremath{\mu}:\PSbr{}\[P\left(\bar{X} - z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \leq \mu \leq \bar{X} + z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right) = 1 - \alpha\]

\PSsubsection{Formula}

\[\text{CI: } \bar{X} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \cdot \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\]

\PSsubsection{Common z-values}

{\def\LTcaptype{none} % do not increment counter
\begin{longtable}[c]{@{}ccc@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
\rowcolor{PSnavy}\PSth{Confidence Level} & \PSth{\ensuremath{\alpha}} & \PSth{z\textsubscript{\ensuremath{\alpha}/2}} \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
90\% & 0.10 & 1.645 \\
95\% & 0.05 & 1.960 \\
99\% & 0.01 & 2.576 \\
\end{longtable}
}

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{ex}{Example}

Noise measurements: n = 36, \ensuremath{\bar{X}} = 0.52V, \ensuremath{\sigma} = 0.12V (known from equipment spec).

95\% CI: 0.52 \ensuremath{\pm} 1.96 \ensuremath{\times} 0.12/\ensuremath{\surd}36 = 0.52 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.0392 = \textbf{[0.481, 0.559] V}

\end{PSbox}

\PSsection{14.4}{CI for Mean — Unknown Variance (t-interval)}

\PSsubsection{The Problem}

Usually \ensuremath{\sigma} is unknown. We estimate it with S. But \ensuremath{\bar{X}}/(S/\ensuremath{\surd}n) does NOT follow N(0,1).

\PSsubsection{Student’s t-Distribution}

\[T = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{S/\sqrt{n}} \sim t(n-1)\]

The t-distribution:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Symmetric about 0
\item
  Heavier tails than N(0,1) (more uncertainty because S is estimated)
\item
  Degrees of freedom: \ensuremath{\nu} = n - 1
\item
  As \ensuremath{\nu} \ensuremath{\to} \ensuremath{\infty}: t(\ensuremath{\nu}) \ensuremath{\to} N(0,1)
\item
  For \ensuremath{\nu} \ensuremath{\ge} 30: t \ensuremath{\approx} z (practically)
\end{itemize}

\PSsubsection{Formula}

\[\text{CI: } \bar{X} \pm t_{\alpha/2, n-1} \cdot \frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}\]

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{ex}{Example}

Component delay: n = 15, \ensuremath{\bar{X}} = 2.34 ns, S = 0.45 ns.

95\% CI: t\_\{0.025, 14\} = 2.145

CI: 2.34 \ensuremath{\pm} 2.145 \ensuremath{\times} 0.45/\ensuremath{\surd}15 = 2.34 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.249 = \textbf{[2.09, 2.59] ns}

(Wider than z-interval because of additional uncertainty in S.)

\end{PSbox}

\PSsection{14.5}{CI for Variance}

\PSsubsection{Setup}

Data from N(\ensuremath{\mu}, \ensuremath{\sigma}\textsuperscript{2}). Want CI for \ensuremath{\sigma}\textsuperscript{2}.

\PSsubsection{Key Distribution}

\[(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2(n-1)\]

\PSsubsection{Formula}

\[\left[\frac{(n-1)S^2}{\chi^2_{\alpha/2, n-1}}, \quad \frac{(n-1)S^2}{\chi^2_{1-\alpha/2, n-1}}\right]\]

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{ex}{Example}

Noise power: n = 20, S\textsuperscript{2} = 0.045 V\textsuperscript{2}.

95\% CI for \ensuremath{\sigma}\textsuperscript{2}: \ensuremath{\chi}\textsuperscript{2}\_\{0.025, 19\} = 32.852, \ensuremath{\chi}\textsuperscript{2}\_\{0.975, 19\} = 8.907

CI: [(19)(0.045)/32.852, (19)(0.045)/8.907] = [0.026, 0.096] V\textsuperscript{2}

95\% CI for \ensuremath{\sigma}: [\ensuremath{\surd}0.026, \ensuremath{\surd}0.096] = [0.161, 0.310] V

\end{PSbox}

\PSsection{14.6}{CI for Proportions}

\PSsubsection{Setup}

n trials, \ensuremath{\hat{x}} = k/n successes. Estimate p = true proportion.

\PSsubsection{Large-Sample Formula (Wald Interval)}

\[\hat{p} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}\]

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{ex}{Example: Bit Error Rate}

Transmitted 100,000 bits, observed 53 errors. \ensuremath{\hat{p}} = 53/100000 = 5.3\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4}.

95\% CI: 5.3\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4} \ensuremath{\pm} 1.96\ensuremath{\surd}(5.3\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4} \ensuremath{\times} 0.9995 / 100000) = 5.3\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4} \ensuremath{\pm} 1.43\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4}

CI: \textbf{[3.9\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4}, 6.7\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4}]}

\end{PSbox}

\PSsection{14.7}{Factors Affecting CI Width}

{\def\LTcaptype{none} % do not increment counter
\begin{longtable}[c]{@{}ccc@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
\rowcolor{PSnavy}\PSth{Factor} & \PSth{Effect on Width} & \PSth{Explanation} \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
\ensuremath{\uparrow} Sample size n & Narrower & More data \ensuremath{\to} more precision \\
\ensuremath{\uparrow} Confidence level & Wider & More confidence \ensuremath{\to} wider net \\
\ensuremath{\uparrow} Variability \ensuremath{\sigma} & Wider & More noise \ensuremath{\to} harder to pin down \ensuremath{\mu} \\
\end{longtable}
}

\PSsubsection{The Tradeoff}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Want narrow CI (precise) \ensuremath{\to} need more data or accept lower confidence
\item
  Want high confidence \ensuremath{\to} accept wider CI or collect more data
\item
  Want both narrow AND high confidence \ensuremath{\to} need LOTS of data
\end{itemize}

\PSsection{14.8}{Sample Size Determination}

\PSsubsection{Question: How many measurements do I need?}

For CI width \ensuremath{\le} 2E (margin of error E):

\[n \geq \left(\frac{z_{\alpha/2} \cdot \sigma}{E}\right)^2\]

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{ex}{Example}

Want to estimate mean delay within \ensuremath{\pm}0.5ms with 95\% confidence. Expect \ensuremath{\sigma} \ensuremath{\approx} 3ms.

n \ensuremath{\ge} (1.96 \ensuremath{\times} 3 / 0.5)\textsuperscript{2} = (11.76)\textsuperscript{2} = 138.3 \ensuremath{\to} need \textbf{n = 139 measurements}

\end{PSbox}

\PSsection{14.10}{Practice Problems}

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{prob}{Problem 1}

A sensor measures voltage. From 50 measurements: \ensuremath{\bar{X}} = 3.32V, S = 0.15V.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
\tightlist
\item
  Construct 95\% CI for \ensuremath{\mu}. (b) Construct 99\% CI for \ensuremath{\mu}. (c) Which is wider and why?
\end{enumerate}

\PSsolution{} 

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
\tightlist
\item
  t\textsubscript{0.025,49} \ensuremath{\approx} 2.010. CI: 3.32 \ensuremath{\pm} 2.010(0.15/\ensuremath{\surd}50) = 3.32 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.043 = [3.277, 3.363]
\item
  t\textsubscript{0.005,49} \ensuremath{\approx} 2.680. CI: 3.32 \ensuremath{\pm} 2.680(0.15/\ensuremath{\surd}50) = 3.32 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.057 = [3.263, 3.377]
\item
  99\% CI is wider — higher confidence requires a wider interval.
\end{enumerate}

\end{PSbox}

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{prob}{Problem 2}

From 25 noise power measurements: S\textsuperscript{2} = 0.048 V\textsuperscript{2}. Construct 95\% CI for \ensuremath{\sigma}\textsuperscript{2}.

\PSsolution{} \ensuremath{\chi}\textsuperscript{2}\textsubscript{0.025,24} = 39.364, \ensuremath{\chi}\textsuperscript{2}\textsubscript{0.975,24} = 12.401\PSbr{}CI: [24(0.048)/39.364, 24(0.048)/12.401] = [0.029, 0.093] V\textsuperscript{2}

\end{PSbox}

\begin{PSbox}[unbreakable]{prob}{Problem 3}

How many BER measurements needed to estimate BER within \ensuremath{\pm}20\% of its true value with 95\% confidence, if expected BER \ensuremath{\approx} 10\textsuperscript{-3}?

\PSsolution{} For proportion: n \ensuremath{\ge} z\textsuperscript{2}p(1-p)/E\textsuperscript{2} where E = 0.2p = 2\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4}.\PSbr{}n \ensuremath{\ge} (1.96)\textsuperscript{2}(10\textsuperscript{-3})(0.999)/(2\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-4})\textsuperscript{2} = 3.84 \ensuremath{\times} 10\textsuperscript{-3} / (4\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-8}) \ensuremath{\approx} 96,000 bits.\PSbr{}Need to transmit approximately 96,000 bits (or observe \textasciitilde{}96 errors).
